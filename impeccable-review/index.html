Scout for Safari: UI/UX review and five refresh directions

A full review of every Scout screen on Mac and iPhone, the main problems it found, and five clearly different looks for the whole app, each shown on the real panel. Nothing in the app has changed yet.

How these were made: panel screens are the real built extension (current main) rendered in Chromium with synthetic data, at the Mac popover size (400×600) and an iPhone sheet size (393 wide). Inter stands in for Apple's SF font, Charter for New York, and Nunito for SF Rounded, because the cloud machine has no Apple fonts. The five directions are CSS and small script layers on top of the real panel (in impeccable-review/option-N/prototype/), not source changes. The Mac and iPhone setup app screens are faithful HTML recreations of OnboardingView.swift, since there is no Xcode here; real Simulator and Safari captures come with the build of whichever direction is chosen. Find coupons and the purchase confirmation are staged with the panel's own markup (no live store run).

2. Top problems (critique and audit)

The automated accessibility audit (axe-core, WCAG 2.2 A/AA) on all 42 captured panel states found one rule failing: the Privacy and Terms links in Settings are 17 px tall on iPhone, under the 24 px minimum. Everything else here comes from design review against Apple's guidelines and the screenshots.

Hierarchy High

  • The new chat screen is mostly empty: a large gray truck and two quiet text lines. Nothing says what Scout can do, and Find coupons and Scout mode are hidden behind + and an unlabeled icon.
  • The composer row holds six controls of the same weight (+, two mode icons, model pill, mic, send). The magnifier for Chat and the truck for Scout are not readable as modes.
  • On Mac the header spends its best space on the full product name next to four equal icons.

Consistency High

  • Three visual languages: the Models sheet is an iOS grouped list, Settings is a long web form with uppercase labels and browser selects, cards and the error box are bordered web boxes.
  • Buttons come in four shapes (8 px rectangles, pills, circles, link text), and primary vs secondary changes by screen.
  • The cyan page glow does not match the blue accent in the panel, and neither matches the green and yellow app icon.

Accessibility Medium

  • The consent dialog shows a focus box around its heading on open; it reads as a bug.
  • The composer opens with a heavy blue focus ring, so the resting state looks selected or in error.
  • iPhone header and answer action buttons are 32 px, under Apple's 44 pt touch target; panel text ignores the iPhone text size setting (fixed px).
  • Settings legal links fail the 24 px target size check on iPhone (axe).

Spacing Medium

  • Left edges do not line up: header 12 px, thread 14 px, composer 10 px, status 16 px.
  • Settings is one 13-section scroll with paragraphs between every control; sections are separated only by hairlines.
  • The status line (cost, "The request failed.") floats between thread and composer, detached from the answer it belongs to.

Typography Medium

  • No real type scale: almost everything is 13 to 16 px at 600 to 650 weight, so headings, labels and body blur together.
  • iPhone body is 14 px, smaller than Apple's 17 pt default reading size.
  • Find coupons attempts mix monospace codes, prices and source links on one wrapping line; the winning code does not stand out.

Color Low

  • Gray plus one Apple blue is safe but anonymous: Scout looks like a generic chat box, with no link to its own icon.
  • The only color on most screens is the store letter tiles and the blue send button.
  • Error state is a red outline box with red text and no way to retry.

Copy Medium

  • "Scout for Safari" is repeated in nearly every sentence (four times in the no-key paragraph). Inside Safari, "Scout" is enough.
  • The no-key screen is a four-sentence billing paragraph before the button.
  • Errors say the same thing twice ("The provider is having problems" plus "The request failed.") and offer no Retry.
  • "Legal (DRAFT)" and "(draft)" links are visible in Settings and the setup app.

Setup app Medium

  • A text-only page: no icon, no picture of the steps, and it never shows whether the extension is on or a key is saved.
  • On iPhone the instructions are a long paragraph of Settings paths with no button.

4. The five directions

Fixes shared by all five, because the audit found them regardless of look: call it "Scout" inside Safari; a real new-chat screen that says what Scout can do; a calm composer focus state; Settings grouped into clear sections; errors with a Retry; the status line moved under its answer; 44 pt targets on iPhone; no focus box on dialog headings; no visible "draft" labels at release; a setup app that shows live status (extension on, key saved).

7. As built: Trailhead by default, five more in Settings

The real panel after the change, captured from the built extension (same Mac/iPhone and Light/Dark switches as above). Trailhead is the default; Settings > Look switches to any of the others at once and remembers it. Classic is the original design. Fonts here are stand-ins for SF Pro Rounded and New York.

6. Recommendation

Option 1, Trailhead. It gives Scout an identity that comes straight from its own green-and-yellow truck icon, sets it apart from ChatGPT, Comet and Safari's own gray UI, and still behaves like an Apple app (system type sizes, standard sheets, light and dark). It suits both everyday questions and the coupon hunt without shouting. If you would rather Scout look like it shipped with Safari, Option 2 (Pure Apple) is the safe second choice.
