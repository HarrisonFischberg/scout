              Permissions

              Every permission here is enforced on this device by Scout itself, never by a server.

              Read the current page

              On by default: Scout sends the page you are on with your question. Off: only tabs you attach.

              Search history and tabs

              Screenshots

              Asked per site: Scout asks before each screenshot of a site, unless you chose Always or Never for it. A screenshot shows everything on screen, including anything typed into the page.

                Scout mode

                On: sending a task in Scout mode starts it at once, and the model may start one when you ask it to do something on the page. While it works, the page glows and shows Take control and Stop; it still asks before anything sensitive. Off also hides the run_task tool from the model.

                Sensitive-action confirmation

                Always on. It cannot be turned off. The check runs on this device from the action and its target, even when the model marks an action as approved.

                "Always allow" sites

                Sites where you chose "Always allow on this site" for a kind of sensitive action. Revoke one to be asked again.

                  Blocked sites

                  Scout never reads, screenshots, or acts on these sites or their subdomains.

                    Blocked by your organization. You cannot change this here.

                      Private browsing

                      Scout works in Private Browsing like in any other tab. Chats in a private tab are deleted from this device when you close the tab, and private tabs never show up in tab search.

                      Shared in conversations

                      What each conversation shared with your AI provider: tabs, screenshots, and tools, by title and site. Clearing removes that record from this device; your provider keeps its own under your account.

                        Button on webpages

                        Handy on iPhone and iPad, where Safari keeps Scout in the page menu. Tap it to open Scout. Drag it up, down, or to the other side. It shows only on sites Scout can access and reads nothing from the page.

                        Side of the screen

                        Toolbar button

                        Shortcut: Control-Shift-S on Mac opens and closes Scout. Safari does not let extensions hide their own button or change the shortcut. To hide the button on Mac, choose View > Customize Toolbar in Safari and drag the Scout truck out. On iPhone and iPad, Scout lives in the page menu (the button in the address bar); turn on Button on webpages above to also get a button on the page.

                        Diagnostics (debug build)

                        Records what the last 5 runs did, on this device only, so you can paste it into a bug report. Never includes page text, API keys, or form values.
