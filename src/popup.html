Scout for Safari

Add your API key to start

Scout for Safari uses your own Anthropic or OpenAI key, kept in your Keychain. Page text you ask about is sent to that provider. Your provider bills your account for every request Scout for Safari makes, at the provider's prices. Scout for Safari shows an estimated cost after each answer or task.

            Permissions

            Every permission here is enforced on this device by Scout for Safari itself, never by a server.

            Read the current page

            On by default: Scout for Safari sends the page you are on with your question. Off: only tabs you attach.

            Search history and tabs

            Screenshots

            Asked per site: Scout for Safari asks before each screenshot of a site, unless you chose Always or Never for it. A screenshot shows everything on screen, including anything typed into the page.

              Scout mode

              On: sending a task in Scout mode starts it at once, and the model may start one when you ask it to do something on the page. While it works, the page glows and shows Take control and Stop; it still asks before anything sensitive. Off also hides the run_task tool from the model.

              Sensitive-action confirmation

              Always on. It cannot be turned off. The check runs on this device from the action and its target, even when the model marks an action as approved.

              "Always allow" sites

              Sites where you chose "Always allow on this site" for a kind of sensitive action. Revoke one to be asked again.

                Blocked sites

                Scout for Safari never reads, screenshots, or acts on these sites or their subdomains.

                  Blocked by your organization. You cannot change this here.

                    Private browsing

                    Scout for Safari works in Private Browsing like in any other tab. Chats in a private tab are deleted from this device when you close the tab, and private tabs never show up in tab search.

                    Shared in conversations

                    What each conversation shared with your AI provider: tabs, screenshots, and tools, by title and site. Clearing removes that record from this device; your provider keeps its own under your account.

                      Button on webpages

                      Handy on iPhone and iPad, where Safari keeps Scout for Safari in the page menu. It stays tucked against the edge; tap it once to bring it out and again to open Scout for Safari. Drag it up, down, or to the other side. It shows only on sites Scout for Safari can access and reads nothing from the page.

                      Side of the screen

                      Toolbar button

                      Shortcut: Control-Shift-S on Mac opens and closes Scout for Safari. Safari does not let extensions hide their own button or change the shortcut. To hide the button on Mac, choose View > Customize Toolbar in Safari and drag the binoculars out. On iPhone and iPad, Scout for Safari lives in the page menu (the button in the address bar); turn on Button on webpages above to also get a button on the page.

                      Diagnostics (debug build)

                      Records what the last 5 runs did, on this device only, so you can paste it into a bug report. Never includes page text, API keys, or form values.
